Exactly what is being compared

Sample both oriented sides every N meters, including their endpoints. Project each sample onto the closest point anywhere on the opposite source polyline, then take that pair’s midpoint. There is no tangent filter, forced forward matching, smoothing, or endpoint snapping. The optional pair-link overlay shows at most 240 evenly selected links; the midpoint line uses every sample.

The union needs an order: the combined line sorts midpoints by the average of their two normalized distances along the source roads. This is an explicit experimental convention. “Both traces, separately” shows the two sample sequences without interleaving them. The unconnected view removes the ordering convention entirely. Decreasing N does not resolve differences between those two traces.

Coordinates use a local WGS84 azimuthal-equidistant metric plane. Sampling, segment projection, and midpoints are computed in that plane (a local approximation to ellipsoidal distances). Ramp inputs already include the existing mainline continuation at staggered joins. The current algorithm retains its existing graph attachment endpoints; the proposed one does not force those endpoints.

This experiment compares geometry on fixed source pairs. It does not change pairing, interchange topology, route selection, or the production map. Runtime measurements cover this pair in a worker, including indexing and turn diagnostics, excluding rendering and the deviation audit. This is not a continental rebuild benchmark.
